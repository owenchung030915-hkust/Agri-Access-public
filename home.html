The gap

A $25 billion credit chasm in Indonesian agriculture

Agriculture employs 30%+ of Indonesia’s workforce — yet 29 million smallholders remain economically invisible, with 72% below the poverty line (World Bank Group, 2020).

Roughly 20 million smallholders (~70%) cannot get formal loans; ~90% lack collateral, and about 20% turn to high-cost informal markets. KUR still asks for 3–5 years of paperwork most farms do not have.

Yield prediction alone cannot underwrite a loan — household shocks, prices, and timing break that shortcut. We separate agronomic risk, cash-flow capacity, and credit risk.

Architecture

Independent data. Separated risk engines.

From farm proof to credit decision support — Sentinel SAR/optical, BMKG / ECMWF / Earth-2 weather, SoilGrids, and cooperative records feed agronomic and cash-flow engines before any credit view.

MVP ships explainable risk evidence. With labelled repayment data, the stack calibrates toward PD and expected loss — still under bank policy and human review.

01 · Identify & farm proof

eKYC, field polygon, crop planting, and loan purpose — the plot becomes machine-readable.

02 · Independent verification

Sentinel SAR/optical, weather ensembles, SoilGrids, and selective drone sensing.

03 · Agronomic risk engine

P10 / P50 / P90 yield, crop-failure probability, weather and soil stress, model uncertainty.

04 · Farm cash-flow engine

Yield × price minus costs → net cash-flow bands and repayment coverage.

05 · Credit decision support

Which loans fit, what’s missing, how to improve — Basel-style parameters for committees.

06 · Bank / licensed PKA

Bank policy + human review → approve / review / reject with amount and conditions.

Why now

The market is open. The law paved the way.

OJK reforms encourage alternative credit scoring and weather-linked data. Open EO and AI weather (Sentinel, Earth-2) lower the cost of farm proof. KUR and AUTP provide subsidized credit and insurance rails — capital is flowing into agriculture.

Agri-Accept sits in that opening: data as collateral for farmers who lack assets, without replacing the lender.

Start in West Java. Scale toward national KUR distribution.

Roadmap

Built for Indonesia first

Pilot with a West Java cooperative to refine explainable models and unit economics, then expand across provinces and integrate with state-owned banks supporting national KUR — targeting the $25B gap.
